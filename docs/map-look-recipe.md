# A map should read as a place at running height

Start with five sentences: what the facility handles, how it moves through the site, where the runner is, one landmark and the view from spawn. Put those relationships into a few large structures before adding props. A pump needs a foot on a real deck and a pipe that connects to another part of the facility. A roof needs columns whose bottoms meet the deck. A support below a deck must reach the water or an actual foundation. Use bearing beams and paired piles for a broad bed rather than one thin leg. Salt beds, bulk salt, connected headers and a loading canopy explain the site better than unrelated boxes.

Keep the running face pale and the underside dark. Check the real face normal, not a generic "top" threshold: Saltglass's 56.31 degree surf face has an upward normal of about 0.555, below Look C's 0.65 threshold. Its local shader uses 0.35–0.52. A slope can be mechanically correct and still be painted like an underside. A shader tied to this map avoids changing original-map presentation.

A look toggle can silently erase your fix. Trace the production material-swap path, including the actual palette alias: Saltglass uses `practice`. Test both states after the host has attached the look. Functional cues should remain legible with either sky/light setup. Native frames caught both clipped pale tops and a dark sea that swallowed the supports; headless shader compilation did not catch either. Keep a clean silhouette, restrained slope seams and a bright sea separate from weathered structure. Quiet interior grids so only real deck boundaries look like gaps. Map-local surface names opt out of generic material replacement; shared contours still follow the collision manifest.

Use orange on the small edge where a choice happens, then on its receiver. Avoid painting every edge or inventing chevrons. Put bands on the measured deck height, not a waypoint's approximate feet height. Test the band centre against the real manifest polygon and its highest overlapping top height. Saltglass’s quay waypoint says 5.6 m where the real takeoff is 5.3 m; its last rising pan overlaps a higher headrace. A receiving surface hidden behind a wall cannot become fully visible through material work; keep that distinction explicit when proposing geometry changes.

Separate presentation proof from movement proof. Pin the delivered geometry hash and compare the actual solid transforms/convex points through both look states. Preserve map ID, runner and recording identity. Keep any geometry experiment on its own branch with its own map and identity, so the art correction can ship separately.

Use identical first-person comparison poses, then input-only traversal captures at running speed. A static eye-height frame proves a view, not traversal or enjoyment. Preserve source SHA, capture settings and failed attempts alongside the selected images so material calibration can be distinguished from accepted results. Give each fresh reviewer the frames, the playtest feedback and the look reference without your design rationale. Fix the visible objection before the next pass.

Before claiming a takeoff view is reachable, get there with ordinary inputs and check real ground support. Saltglass’s first edge peek fell; a later grounded peek still had its receiver sightline blocked by the store. Those are different results. A camera placed beyond the support cannot settle the discovery question.

Use `tools/shot.py` only after the integrator announces it, through the shared lock and private display. Keep images, concepts and receipts outside Git. Run the fast component check before runtime commits, retain small receipts, and remove its temporary source/profile after reading them. Full original-map parity belongs to integration for this run.

For a preserved course, replay a sealed human run read-only as stronger motion evidence. Warm up the host, restart through its public method, feed events before the motor at 128 Hz, and apply time zero before tick one. Replay raw keyboard/mouse events once. Derived action diagnostics can omit modifier-dependent edges; they are not always a complete input tape. Require exact sampled positions when identities match. Saltglass’s art revision reproduced all 4,786 samples of one played finish with zero error; that protects the played line, while the integration parity gate covers the original-map matrix.

Export the actual worst replay error and compared coverage. A counter that ignores differences below 0.00001 m cannot justify an exact-match claim. The final Saltglass outside verifier requires and reports worst error 0.0 for its 4,100-tick prefix; the separate inland replay compares all 4,786 ticks. Keep those scopes distinct.
