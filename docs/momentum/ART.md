# Momentum art kit

A small reusable surface kit for the new hop-focused Spillway course. It contains four CC0 scan sets from Poly Haven, one shader that maps them in metres without mesh UVs, a cached material API, and one original prop (an edge railing). It has no dependency on the R12 world. Engine choice is still open: the PNG textures and the glTF props are the portable sources, and the Godot shader and script are a provisional adapter.

## Using it

```gdscript
const Materials := preload("res://scripts/momentum_materials.gd")

mesh.material_override = Materials.get_material(&"deck")          # cached, shared
var wet := Materials.variant(&"old_concrete", {"waterline": 2.5}) # edit-safe copy
var rail: Node3D = Materials.instance_prop(&"rail_section")       # mesh + collision proxy
```

- `get_material(kind)` returns a shared `ShaderMaterial`. Kinds: `deck`, `concrete`, `old_concrete`, `steel`, `ochre`, `brick`. Every kind uses the same shader, so the kit compiles one shader.
- `variant(kind, overrides)` and `build(kind, overrides)` return uncached copies. Overrides are shader parameters, or `top`/`side` set names for trials.
- `instance_prop(prop, collision := true)` instantiates `rail_section` or `rail_post`, applies the ochre material, and adds a `CollisionProxy` StaticBody3D.

Plain `BoxMesh` geometry with `size` set (or node scale) is enough. Mapping uses each object's rotation frame in metres, so a pitched or yawed slope keeps true scale with its joints parallel to its own edges. Neighbours with the same rotation continue the pattern without a seam. Faces sloping less than about 48° (world normal.y above `top_min_up`, 0.66) use the top set, steeper faces use the side set, and a narrow band blends the two. One merged mesh still gets metre-true texels per face, but its joints follow the merged mesh's frame rather than each original box.

## Kinds and value ladder

Values are measured in the native study under the R12 art-pass sun. Rendered sRGB means from `16-stills-kit`:

| kind | top faces | side faces | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| deck | brushed concrete, cool grey, about 177 | formwork lifts | running and landing surfaces; the lightest plane in view |
| concrete | same scan, about 152 | formwork, about 145 | structure, lower decks, piers |
| old_concrete | darker, wider tone variation | green-grey with run-off stain, about 95 | older pours, wet bases (`waterline`) |
| steel | dark blue-grey riveted plate, metallic | same | bridges, girders, gates |
| ochre | restrained ochre paint over the plate, sparse chips | same | rails, nosings, hardware accents |
| brick | concrete cap | warm red brick | housings and gatehouses |

Deck tops have 12 mm saw-cut joints on a 4 m grid. Each bay gets a small tone step and its own texture offset under the joint, so a broad floor shows no repeat grid. Side faces have vertical monolith joints every 12 m with a tone step per pour. A low-frequency macro tone (11 m and 30 m) breaks repetition at running distance. Joints thinner than a pixel keep their average darkness instead of flickering.

## Assets

Full provenance is in `assets/momentum/MANIFEST.json`: URLs, API md5s, upstream and derived SHA-256s, physical size, modifications and import settings. Licence copies are in `assets/momentum/licences/`.

| set | Poly Haven asset | metres per repeat | used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| deck | Brushed Concrete (`brushed_concrete`) | 2.5 | top faces of every masonry kind |
| formwork | Concrete Layers 02 (`concrete_layers_02`) | 2.0 | concrete side faces |
| plate | Metal Plate 02 (`metal_plate_02`) | 2.0 | steel and ochre |
| brick | Red Brick (`red_brick`) | 1.4 | brick side faces |

All four are CC0 1.0, downloaded as 1K PNG and checked against the API md5s. The only change is a reduction to 8-bit RGB without alpha or metadata; nothing is resampled. Normal maps are the `nor_gl` (OpenGL) variants. Godot imports albedo and ARM as BC1 with mipmaps, and normals as two-channel RGTC with mipmaps; the shader rebuilds z. Each ARM map's roughness channel is limited by its normal map (Godot's `roughness/src_normal`), which works as specular anti-aliasing in the mips. The 2K and larger upstream versions were not downloaded. At running speed and 1280×720, a 2 m repeat at 1K is about 2 mm per texel, finer than a pixel at 2 m away.

**Props.** `rail_section.gltf` is a 2 m section: a 60 mm post at the origin, a 48 mm top rail with its top at 1.094 m, a knee rail at 0.55 m, and a base plate. It has 80 triangles. `rail_post.gltf` closes a run with one post (24 triangles). Both are original work generated by `assets/momentum/props/make_rail.py`, with +x along the run, +y up, metres, and origin at the post foot on the deck. The collision proxy is one box over the whole visual envelope, including the base plate: 2.08 × 1.1 × 0.16 m centred at (0.96, 0.55, 0) for a section, and 0.16 × 1.1 × 0.16 m for a post. Root enlarged those proxies during integration; the original study used the thinner barrier proxy. Nothing fits under the knee rail anyway. A course that wants rails as hop-over obstacles rather than barriers has to decide that deliberately: the top is at 1.1 m.

## Costs

These were measured in the native study on llvmpipe and are relative only.

- **Draw calls:** identical to flat and R12 materials in every view (138 on the run-up view). The kit adds no draws or objects.
- **Texture memory:** imported desktop textures total about 11.2 MB for the four sets (a set is 0.70 MB albedo, 1.40 MB normal and 0.70 MB ARM). Godot's reported texture memory rose 8.4 MB over the flat baseline in the study.
- **Git:** about 18 MB of PNGs.
- **Shader:** a face samples only its own set (three textures) plus one value noise. The fragment cost on llvmpipe is about 1.6× R12's procedural concrete on the run-up view (about 145 against 88 ms host frame). That is not a GPU or frame-rate claim.
- **Props:** 104 triangles for a section plus an end post, one material, and one draw per instance (no instancing set up).

## Evidence and checks

Everything is outside Git under `checkpoints/spillway/r13/material-kit/`. The study tool is `tools/capture-momentum-materials.gd`. It builds an isolated in-memory fixture labelled "MATERIAL STUDY … not a route": a 48 m run-up deck, formwork and old walls, an 18° slope, a lower deck, a steel bridge with rails, brick, and a slab both yawed and pitched. It renders in three surface modes: `flat` (R12 palette as plain colours), `r12` (R12 procedural shaders) and `kit`. The camera is an ordinary first-person camera on a plain walk/hop study body, driven by parsed mouse motion and pressed actions. That body is not a motor candidate.

- `--mode=normals` lights synthetic OpenGL dome normals from image-right and image-up on a floor, a wall and the yawed and pitched slab, and asserts that the lit half is brighter. It then compares the imported RGTC formwork normal with the same PNG decoded raw at runtime.
- `--mode=stills` renders 13 close and running-distance views. Four of them are rendered again turned by a quarter pixel, as an aliasing comparison.
- `--mode=motion` saves every frame of a 60 fps run from the run-up to the brick pier.

Motion results (`17-motion-*`) came from one tape with identical trajectories in all three modes: 740 frames, run-up to brick pier at 9 m/s, 16 hops. A temporal second difference, |f(t−1) + f(t+1) − 2f(t)| / 2 in grey levels out of 255, measures flicker. It is meaningful where content moves less than about a pixel per frame:

| band | flat | R12 | kit |
|---|---|---|---|
| run-up ground 12–35 m ahead (frames 30–246) | 1.39 | 3.59 | 2.94 |
| ground 20 m+ ahead, whole run | 0.60 | 1.44 | 1.29 |
| formwork wall beside the run-up | 0.12 | 1.56 | 3.19 |

At distance the kit is calmer than R12's procedural concrete. Its higher wall figure is real relief sliding past at several pixels per frame. Enlarged consecutive crops show stable features, while R12's board lines break into dashes, which is actual aliasing. Joints stay continuous lines at running distance, and rail posts keep their shape. The side-by-side video is `motion-flat-r12-kit.mp4`.

Runs use the pinned Godot 4.7.2 from `tools/check-spillway.sh` with Compatibility rendering, on a private Xvfb `:154`, with Dummy audio and an isolated XDG profile. Imports and renders happen in a scratch mirror of the checkout, so the shared `.godot` cache and other agents' files are untouched.

## Rejected

- `concrete_floor_worn_001` as the deck: too uniform, rendering as blank plastic once brightened to deck value.
- `concrete` 4 m vertical board-form for walls: strong close up, but it nearly doubled the quarter-pixel change on the running wall view (1.43 against 0.77), and it is only 4 mm per texel at 1K.
- `blue_metal_plate`: sits too close to the water hue.
- `red_brick_03`: higher contrast and more shimmer at distance.
- `concrete_slab_wall_02`: reads as a timber fence when tiled.
- External props: Poly Haven's models were considered. The industrial ones are 4k–90k triangles and not dam hardware, so an original rail was cheaper and more useful.

## Weaknesses

- Under a static quarter-pixel turn, kit frames change about 5× more than R12 frames on the run-up view (0.68 against 0.14 of 255; flat 0.06). Most of that is real texture gradient, and the moving sequences above show no sparkle. However, llvmpipe's filtering is not a GPU's, and anisotropic filtering quality varies by driver, so a GPU check of the far deck is still owed.
- The deck scan still repeats faintly within a 4 m bay where its darker blotch recurs. Per-bay offsets hide it across bays, not inside one.
- The paint, stain and macro noise are procedural and value-based. They read as tone, not as specific decals or wear.
- Top/side selection is by normal only. A wall top narrower than a bay still gets deck joints, and a surface under about 48° counts as a floor.
- The water in the study is a plain fixture plane, not a kit material. The kit has no water, foliage or terrain.
